 I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A.      Project status

1.      The Water and Sanitation Development Project (WSDP) was approved by the Board on April 26, 2017 and became
effective on March 9, 2018. The closing date is October 31, 2022. The total IDA financing for the project is US$300 million
equivalent, consisting of an International Development Association (IDA) credit on regular terms in the amount of SDR
27.4 million (equivalent to US$37 million) and an IDA Scale-Up Facility credit in the amount of Euro 248.1 million
(equivalent to US$263 million).

2.       The slow start to project implementation was due to delays in meeting project effectiveness conditions,
bottlenecks in transferring funds to the implementing agencies, and a lack of detailed designs for some critical
infrastructure investments. The project is the first World Bank-financed water and sanitation operation to be
implemented under the devolved structure under which counties and their water services providers (WSPs) are
responsible for implementing county-specific project activities. A new water act was enacted in 2016 that specified the
roles of the water sector institutions and the county governments in the regulation, management, development, and
delivery of water and sanitation services consistent with the 2010 Constitution. However, the transition of responsibilities
from the previous dispensation to the current one has been slow and institutional mandates are still being refined.
Moreover, all but one implementing agency (the Coast Water Works Development Agency) are implementing a Bank-
financed project for the first time and needed to understand how to comply with the Bank’s policies and procedures.
Finally, one component of the project is intended to assist historically marginalized north-eastern counties, where capacity
for implementation of project activities is limited.

3.      Implementation progress improved significantly in 2020. Disbursements totaled only US$13.4 million on
December 31, 2019 but reached US$61.9 million on March 31, 2021. As at May 2021, contracts totaling $177 million have
been fully awarded, with another batch of US$22m at advanced procurement stages and expected to be awarded by July

2021. This will see a total of 67 percent of the Credit committed. A final batch of activities are at advanced design stage.
As a result, disbursements are projected to rise significantly over the remaining project implementation period.

4.       The project has been rated moderately unsatisfactory (MU) for overall implementation progress (IP) and for
progress towards achievement of the Project Development Objectives (PDOs) over the past 18 months. This is due to the
slow progress of implementation and the risk that the original PDO targets may not be achieved within the current project
period. The Government of Kenya (GoK) is committed to the project and has now complied with all the covenants specified
in the Financing Agreement. Compliance with environmental and social safeguards has been rated moderately
satisfactory. There are no outstanding fiduciary and safeguard issues. An assessment of the project’s financial
management arrangements, including compliance with the legal covenants related to financial management, found that
they provide reasonable assurance that the project funds are used for the intended purposes. The project account has
been audited for the fiscal years ending June 30, 2019 and 2020 and an unqualified report has been issued for each year.
Recent Implementation Status and Results (ISR) reports rates financial management and procurement as moderately
satisfactory (MS).

5.      An action plan has been agreed with the Client to upgrade IP and PDO ratings. The Bank team has been closely
monitoring implementation of the action plan to quickly identify issues and seek solutions to challenges. The plan is largely
on track, and the project is likely to be upgraded following the next implementation support mission in May 2021 if
implementation continues to improve. Key milestones are presented in the attached matrix of agreed actions.

6.      The project has been restructured twice, to address implementation bottlenecks and to respond to the Covid-19
pandemic. The first restructuring approved by the Bank in January 2020 adjusted the project's institutional arrangements
aimed at accelerating implementation. This resulted in improved coordination between the national and county-level
governments, allowing for the transfer of funds to WSPs to implement agreed activities. The second restructuring
approved by the Bank in July 2020 broadened the project’s scope to include interventions to strengthen the financial
performance of WSPs affected by the Covid-19 pandemic and to enhance their capacity to provide safe and reliable water
services during and after the pandemic. Some US$50 million was reallocated to support a Conditional Liquidity Support
Grant (CLSG) facility to this end. The first tranche of about US$30 million was disbursed in December 2020.

B.      Rationale for restructuring

7.      During the midterm review that took place in September/October 2020, the Bank and the implementing agencies
assessed the likelihood that envisaged contracts could be executed within the project implementation period. They
agreed that contracts not likely to be completed by the project closing date of October 31, 2021 would be reviewed for
cancellation at the next mission. These include contracts estimated at the time to total about US$93.6, for fecal sludge
management facilities (components 1 and 2), pilot onsite sanitation facilities and water supply (component 2), and water
supply (component 3). Some of the funds would be considered for reallocation to a second phase of the CLSG facility,
depending on performance of WSPs and demand for the facility.

8.      The rationale for the restructuring is to revise the results framework to reflect the resulting changes to the project
scope. Through a letter dated February 9, 2021, the GoK requested the Bank in consultation with the implementing
agencies to revise the results framework to reflect activities that can realistically be achieved by the project closing date.
An additional restructuring is planned following the implementation support mission to take place in May 2021 to
reallocate or cancel the funds that will not be used for currently envisaged activities.

 II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

9.      The proposed changes are to the results framework to reflect what the project can achieve. No changes are
proposed to the PDO or the safeguard category, and no new safeguard policy will be triggered. Therefore, the proposed
restructuring is a level 2 restructuring. Details of the proposed changes are presented below.

10.       One PDO level indicator has been dropped. This is “People provided with access to improved sanitation services
under the project—urban.” This indicator was intended to reflect investments in improved sanitation in the north-eastern
counties, which are no longer expected to take place. Four intermediate indicators—(a) quantity of septic sludge treated
in facilities supported under the project (component 1), (b) new piped household water connections that are resulting
from the project intervention (component 2), (c) quantity of septic sludge treated in facilities supported under the project
(component 2), and (d) results-based financing mechanism established and operational (component 3)—have been
dropped because the associated investments will not be carried out.

11.     Targets for some indicators have been revised, based on information from ongoing and anticipated contracts. The
outcome target for “people in urban areas provided with access to improved water sources under the project” has been
increased from 66,050 to 89,200 to reflect increases in the number of community water points to be constructed in the
north-eastern counties. The target for “people benefiting under the project from a connection to the sewage system or
from improved septic sludge management” has been reduced from 240,000 to 130,000 because no septic sludge
management facilities will be constructed under the project. The revised results framework is presented in Annex 1. A
table describing the changes to the results framework is presented in Annex 2.
