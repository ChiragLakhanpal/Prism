 Implementation Status and Key Decisions

  The project had a slow start due to multiple factors, including delays in meeting project effectiveness conditions, bottlenecks in funds flow
  processes, and the lack of implementation readiness for critical infrastructure investments. The project is the first World Bank-financed water and
  sanitation operation to be implemented under the devolved structure where counties and their water services providers (WSPs) are responsible for
  implementing county-specific project activities. In January 2020, at the request of the Government of Kenya (GoK), the World Bank approved a
  restructuring of the project involving, among others, adjustments to the project's institutional arrangements to accelerate implementation. The
  restructuring has helped to improve implementation progress. The main challenge of funds flow to the counties has been resolved. In addition,
  both the project steering committee and the technical committee have been constituted. In May 2020, the GoK requested a second restructuring to
  broaden the scope of the project to include interventions to strengthen the financial performance of WSPs affected by COVID-19 pandemic and to
  enhance their capacity to provide safe and reliable water services during and after the pandemic. Some US$50 million was reallocated to support a
  conditional liquidity grant facility. The restructuring has been completed and the grant is expected to be disbursed to WSPs between October and
  December 2020.

  Overall progress implementation has improved. The pace of implementation of project activities under Components 1 and 2 is picking up. To date,
  contracts worth about US$60 million (20 percent of the Credit) have been committed (signed/awarded). About half of these contracts are at the
  mobilization stage, although construction schedules have been affected by the current GoK restrictions related to the COVID-19 pandemic. Another
  set of contracts worth about US$40 million is at advanced stage of procurement, expected to be signed by end of October 2020. However, most of
  the implementing agencies (IAs) have encountered delays in awarding the contracts for consulting firms to undertake design review and supervision
  of the immediate works contracts, and to prepare detailed designs for any additional identified medium and long-term interventions. The technical
  assistance firm to be hired by MoWSI to support the IAs on key issues such as procurement, safeguards, financial management, evaluation M&E
  and performance assessment is also significantly delayed but is at the final stage of procurement.
   The IAs have continued to receive capacity building and training in key areas including financial management, procurement, and safeguards
  management to ensure that they can implement the project effectively.
