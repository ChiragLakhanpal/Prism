Strata Blueprint

Strata links what happened in World Bank-financed procurement to the documents that explain how and why. A procurement specialist checking a borrower's plan sees how comparable packages went, what went wrong, and what helped, with every claim cited to a paragraph and page.

The idea

One question, three strata of evidence, one cited answer

Today the three strata sit in separate systems. A specialist who wants to know why similar packages slipped has to rebuild the link by hand. The World Bank's own study of procurement delays did exactly that, through what it called "extensive manual review" of aide-memoires, plans and completion reports. Strata makes that link once, keeps it current, and shows its work.

About 22% of 656 works contracts in STEP were signed on their planned date, and 28% were signed more than 270 days late. Bid evaluation was the longest stage.World Bank procurement delay study, documents.worldbank.org (099062824153516589)

IEG found the PPSD is "typically a generic document with similar content across many projects", with market analysis that lacks operationally useful information.IEG, Making Procurement Work Better

Domain check · how the work actually happens

Where Strata enters the work

The borrower owns the PPSD and the Procurement Plan. "The Borrower shall prepare the PPSD" (Regulations 4.3), "one single PPSD should be prepared for all contracts" (Short Form guidance 2.2), and the plan lives in STEP. The World Bank's procurement specialist supports the borrower, assesses the implementing agency's procurement risk, clears the plan and writes the procurement section of the Project Paper. Strata follows that split: implementing agencies draft with it, World Bank staff review with it, and nothing Strata produces is a World Bank decision.Procurement Regulations, 7th edition (2025); Bank Procedure on procurement (2026); PPSD guidance and templates (2024)

What each person does first

  • Implementing agency: "Check my plan before I submit", on a private upload of a STEP export or on the latest published version.
  • World Bank procurement specialist: "Check a plan" on the borrower's latest version, then review notes and suggested evidence for the PPSD.
  • Task team leader: a mission brief with the latest plan per agency, awards since the last ISR, the rating history, disbursement and a closing check.

Formats Strata writes in

  • PPSD risk table, Short Form, the default: Risk Description · Description of mitigation · Risk Owner.
  • Long Form, when a contract crosses the guidance's Table I thresholds: adds Likelihood and Impact (1 to 5), the Overall Risk Score and the Procurement Process Stage.
  • STEP plan row: STEP's own columns, with a planned date for every step. STEP assigns references and holds actual dates.
  • Strata writes descriptions and mitigations with counts and sources as footnotes. Risk owners and ratings stay with people.

What Strata cannot see, and says so

The prior-review queue, complaints, contract amendments, contract management reports and post reviews are restricted and stay in STEP. A borrower's PPSD and draft plans are Official Use Only, so uploads stay with the uploader's team and are never shown to anyone else. The demo uses published plans only.

Module 1 · ingestion and representation

How documents and records become evidence

Passages are numbered paragraphs

IEG itself cites "ICR para 54", so the numbered paragraph is the citation unit, the way a speaker turn was in your transcript search at NSR. Long paragraphs split at sentence ends. Tables become typed rows plus one summary passage that never backs a number. Every passage keeps its project, document type, public date, section, paragraph number, PDF page and printed page.

Suppliers resolve on keys first

The STEP supplier ID matched across award notices and the contracts table in 7 of 7 checks, so it comes first. Names are matched only where no ID exists, and uncertain pairs go to a person. A joint venture stays one supplier linked to its members. Individual consultants are never shown by name.

Lessons keep their quote

A batch model reads each completion report and IEG review once. Every lesson keeps its verbatim quote and page, and the check rejects rather than repairs. A real one from the showcase project: "Base works estimates on market rates to avoid rebidding", backed by ICR P130164 ¶54, page 23, where "108 of 328 cases had to go through rebidding".

The stress shelf

Five hard documents, shown on the Readiness page and in the demo:

  • The Rajasthan roads PAD (P130164): a 100-page scan with a garbled two-column risk annex
  • The longest PAD in the slice
  • A procurement plan with hundreds of rows, whose columns scramble in plain text
  • A completion report in French
  • A document over 500 pages, if one exists, split past the parser's limit

Module 3 · guardrails and attribution · ADR 0003 Decided

How an answer gets checked before anyone sees it

Numbers come from tools only

The model never computes. If a derived figure helps, a tool returns it as a column. This is fairshare's rule, extended to dates, which fairshare's verifier skipped.

Documents are data, never instructions

Retrieved passages are scanned by Prompt Shields when they are ingested and again when they are retrieved. The agent's rules live in its Foundry instructions and messages carry data only, which is the fix for the false refusals fairshare hit on October 15.

Why the tools run in the app ADR 0003

A Foundry-hosted agent calling Databricks directly would use one shared token. Every viewer would see the same data, and the verifier would not hold the rows. Running the tools in the app keeps both.

Module 3 · disagreeing evidence

When reports disagree, both stay on screen

Judging how a project turned out

1
ICR Review or PPAR (IEG)
2
Completion report (ICR)
3
Last ISR
4
Appraisal (PAD)

Independent and retrospective sources outrank ratings made along the way. IEG ratings average about 15 percentage points below the World Bank's own final self-assessment (Geli, Kraay and Nobakht, 2014, WPS 7001).

Facts about a moment

The record made at that moment wins: the PAD for appraisal ratings, each ISR for its own date, contracts and plans for dates and amounts. Later retellings are kept and marked.

Ties go to the more specific source, then the more recent. A report re-issued under the same number replaces its earlier version.

How a disagreement is shown

  • Both sources in the order they were written, with equal weight.
  • One is tagged "Strata leans on this", with the rule in one sentence and a line saying what changes if you keep the other.
  • Amber only when it changes a number or claim in your answer or draft. Otherwise a blue "Sources differ" note: ISR and IEG ratings differ often, and constant warnings get ignored. Never red.
  • Shown only beside the claim it concerns.
Two reports disagree · real example: P130164, Rajasthan Road Sector Modernization
ISR 7 · August 26, 2016 · written during the projectProgress towards achievement of PDO: Satisfactory, three months after the May 2016 mid-term review.
ICR ¶78 · October 10, 2019 · written after closing · Strata leans on this"it was clearly identified at the MTR that the achievement of part of the PDO was compromised by the major delays in implementing activities under Components 2 and 3"

For a judgment of how the project went, Strata leans on the retrospective report and keeps the ISR as the record of what was rated at the time. You can keep the ISR instead, with a reason that goes into the evidence pack.

Not a disagreement: ISRs 1 to 9 rated the objective Satisfactory, ISRs 10 and 11 Moderately Satisfactory, and ISRs 12 and 13 Moderately Unsatisfactory. A rating that moves over time is a trajectory.

Numbers in words

Name the reference class ("of 37 comparable packages"). One denominator per comparison ("in 10"). Under 50 cases, show "31 of 37" beside "about 8 in 10". No percentage or range from fewer than 20 cases, and the rule also covers subgroups in drivers and what-ifs. When Strata holds back, it says so in the first person: "I'm not giving a range: only 12 comparable packages. Here are all 12."

A track record, not a confidence score

Under every time estimate: "Of 118 packages from 2025 that Strata had not seen, 93 finished inside its 8-in-10 range; a well-sized range catches about 94. Most misses ran late." The numbers here are illustrative. Rolling backtests produce the real ones: train before a date, test on the next 12 months, for 2021 to 2025.

What-ifs on fair levers only

Users can compare market approach, prequalification and lotting. Matching dots light up with their count, a ghost shows the previous estimate, and the copy says "went with", never "caused". Strata never suggests splitting a contract to stay under a review threshold.

Design rationale

Why the screens look the way they do

Strata's users are experts who have to defend their advice to a borrower or a manager, and a wrong number costs them credibility. Its first readers are a data and AI panel. So every design choice aims at three things, in this order: the right decision, trust that matches the evidence, and speed. Five rules carry that, and each rests on named research.

Trust, not over-trust

Say what was checked, and what was not

The check line is neutral: "Checked: 23 of 23 numbers match records; 14 of 14 quotes are on their pages. Not checked: whether these are the right comparables." A green banner reads as "safe", and explanations raise acceptance whether or not the answer is right (Bansal et al. 2021).

The first removed sentence stays in view

The removed box opens under "In short" and shows the first sentence the checks removed, with its reason. Hiding it behind a link would turn a trust feature into a footnote.

How you checked is recorded

Citations you opened show a visited state, and the draft bar says "You opened 3 of 14 sources", saved with the evidence pack (Skitka, Mosier and Burdick 2000). Forcing functions appear only where the person commits: the plan date before the range, risk owners and ratings, disagreements resolved before export (Buçinca, Malaya and Gajos 2021).

Signals, not scores

A signal is a fixed check with a stated reason, and each one shows its track record. Equal-weight rules hold up well (Dawes 1979), and a list of reasons is harder to over-trust than a single score.

Showing uncertainty honestly

Dots, not curves

Each dot is one real comparable package. Countable dots led to better decisions than density curves or plain intervals (Kay et al. 2016; Fernandes et al. 2018), small samples should show their points (Weissgerber et al. 2015), and a real dot can be opened. Above about 60 comparables, the chart switches to 20 quantile dots.

Two lanes, one axis

The past range (what happened to comparable packages) and the time estimate (Strata's adjusted range for this package) sit in separate lanes on one month axis, because position on a common scale is read most accurately (Cleveland and McGill 1984). The estimate is a nested bar, like the bands in the World Bank's Global Economic Prospects fan charts.

The plan is a line, not a dot

A ring among the dots reads as one more outcome. A line through both lanes reads as a decision, and Strata reads off the count at that point: "4 of 37 were signed within your 6 months." The line can be dragged, with keyboard and number input.

Open packages count

Packages not yet signed show as hollow dots, and the ranges use Kaplan-Meier, so the slowest cases are not quietly dropped. Plotting only finished packages would make every range look shorter than it is.

Domain check · vocabulary

Words a procurement specialist would use

Package, PR, PO, RFQ or RFP? PR and PO are corporate purchasing terms. "PR" appears nowhere in the Regulations, the Bank Procedure, the PPSD guidance or STEP, and "purchase order" only for UN-agency purchases and framework call-offs. RFB, RFQ and RFP are selection methods: the Regulations define each one "as a selection method", so they say how a package goes to market, not what it is. The unit itself has no single official name. STEP calls a plan row an activity, the Regulations reason per contract, the Chief Procurement Officer's 2025 deck says "contract package size", and Bangladesh's procurement law says "package" too. So Strata keeps "package" and always shows the STEP activity reference beside it.Term counts and quotes from the Regulations (7th edition), the Bank Procedure (2026), the PPSD guidance (2024), the delay study and the P156634 STEP export

Objects, not stages

Navigating by lifecycle stage would mirror the assessment, but planning and tender design share one package page, a project belongs to monitoring and learning at once, and implementing agencies and the public don't think in stages. STEP, the Operations Portal and projects.worldbank.org are organized by project and activity (Jakob's law). The stages still show where they help: the first-run Home, eyebrows on package sections, a lessons filter.

The project page is the hub

Every source joins on the P-code, and every user starts from a project. Every P-code in Strata links to the project page, and a pasted P-code goes straight there. Its tabs copy projects.worldbank.org (Summary, Procurement, Documents) and add two: Timeline, where What, How and Why meet in date order, and Connections, a one-hop neighbourhood of at most about 20 nodes.

Five nouns, no verbs

Nouns navigate and verbs act. Five distinct nouns carry more information scent than four with one vague label, and five is DESIGN.md's cap. "Ask" and "Delegate" were verbs, and "Delegate" means delegated clearance authority at the World Bank, so both are gone.

One box, the same place

The box sits in a white band under the navy bar on every page, and "/" focuses it. As you type, it lists what Enter will do ("Open P130164 · Check this plan · Ask this"), so a question never silently becomes a package. On a project or package page it carries a scope chip you can remove.

Strata reads only public data, so every view sees the same records. Views change what Strata adds on top (signals, estimates, drafts) and what you can do. A pill at the right of the navy bar ("View: World Bank staff ▾") adds ?view=piu or ?view=public to the URL and a banner that never collapses: "Preview of what an implementing agency officer at NWWDA sees. You are still signed in as yourself." The server applies that view's rules, a preview can only take things away, and checking and exporting are disabled in previews.

The first 60 seconds

Reviewers land as World Bank staff on a Home with seeded examples, labeled as examples, and a Start here strip with one example per stage: check a published plan (P156634), plan a package from a sentence, see a project's whole story (P130164), see a lesson change, check the data, see the public view. Every page states its purpose under its title, so someone arriving from a link is never stranded.

The deck

The deck stays at /present with nothing in the app linking to it, as you decided: a working tool carries no demo scaffolding. Its URL goes in the proposal and the README, so reviewers exploring alone can still find it.

Small screens, slow links

Below 768 px a labeled Menu holds the tabs, the drawer becomes a full-screen sheet, charts lead with their sentence, tables become cards and the neighbourhood becomes a list. Home and project summaries render from precomputed records before any model call, and every route is tested on a throttled 3G connection.

A specialist's path

One procurement specialist, from a changed plan to exported drafts

  1. Home

    Monday. One plan changed: the Northern Water Works Development Agency's October 9 plan for the Kenya water project. One activity needs a look and two are worth watching.

  2. Check a plan

    Every activity is checked against past ranges, review thresholds and its own dates. KE-NWWDA-541055-CW-RFB passed its planned completion with no signed contract, nine days before the project closes.

  3. Package workspace

    For a new roads package the borrower is planning, the specialist sees the past range of comparable packages as dots, the borrower's plan as a line through them, and a planned date for each STEP step.

  4. Evidence drawer

    Reads a risk and what helped, then opens ICR ¶54 of the Rajasthan project, page 23, beside the answer. IEG's review restates the same lesson.

  5. Project timeline

    Opens P130164's timeline and sees ISR 7's "Satisfactory" next to the ICR's later account of the same mid-term review, with the rule Strata applied.

  6. Drafts

    Sets the risk owners, marks the suggested PPSD rows and the review notes as checked, and exports a Word file and a STEP row. Nothing is sent.

  7. Projects you follow

    Before the next mission, the brief lists lapsed dates, an amount far above its estimate and a late direct-selection activity.

  8. Lessons

    A new completion report narrows a lesson used in the draft. The row shows was and now, with "Update this row" ready.

Screens · wireframes with their reasoning

Nine screens, and why each looks the way it does

Wireframes follow DESIGN.md. The dashed box in each navy bar stands for the World Bank logo. Rows, numbers, dates and quotes are real where a source is named; the rest are marked illustrative. The deck opens at /present and no screen links to it.

Why this layout

A briefing made by fixed checks, then the work waiting for you. A blank chat box would make experts type before they get anything, and an AI-wary reader's first screen would be text a model wrote. The old tiles and sidebar are gone: between them they offered four routes to the same list.

Laws at work

BLUF: the heading is the answer, and its counts match the lines below. Serial position: your own work comes first. Hick's law and Occam's razor: two blocks, one action per line. Recognition over recall: suggested starts. Zeigarnik: what waits for you is a short list that empties.

Domain check

The lines are about plans, activities and STEP references, the units staff already work in. "No model wrote this" sits on the block it describes, not under the box, which does call a model. Drafts are "to check", never "to approve".

Why this layout

A table, because every activity has the same attributes and people compare down columns. The findings sit in the last column, where each row's reading ends, and rows with no signals fold away. One primary action drafts review notes on the rows that matter.

Laws at work

Jakob's law: STEP's own column names and values, word for word. Pareto: one row needs a look out of eight. Von Restorff: only the exceptions get a mark. Visibility of system status: what Strata cannot see is stated on the page.

Domain check

The borrower owns the plan. Strata marks rows and drafts review notes, and never edits STEP. Delay is measured from the first published planned date, because plan updates re-date steps. Data notes are shown rather than hidden: that is data readiness in context.

Why this layout

One narrative column, because the reading order is the argument's order: what Strata understood, the answer, the evidence, then the options. Tabs would hide the risks, and a chat transcript can't be rescanned or exported. A sticky header keeps the check status and the one primary action in reach while the column scrolls.

Laws at work

Tesler's law: one sentence becomes ten editable criteria. Split attention: the comparison sits in the chart header, beside what it changes. Doherty threshold: what-ifs move within 100 ms from a precomputed grid. Progressive disclosure: sections 2, 4 and 5 open on demand. Von Restorff: one solid button.

Domain check

Chips use STEP's fields. Review type and market approach are computed from the thresholds, not typed: US$15 million of works is prior review, above the US$10 million line. Sections follow the PPSD: risks (section 3) come before approach options (section 6). The action suggests rows for the borrower's PPSD, because the borrower owns it.

Why this layout

A drawer that leaves the page usable, so the claim and its source stay on screen together and closing returns you to your place. A modal would hide the claim, a new page would lose it, and an embedded PDF viewer is slow and hard for screen readers. Its width is fixed at about 65 characters a line.

Laws at work

Split-attention effect (Chandler and Sweller 1992): claim and source side by side. Figure-ground and continuity: the selected risk stays highlighted on the left. Progressive disclosure: the drawer, then the PDF page. Recognition over recall: the matching words are highlighted.

Domain check

The citation unit is the numbered paragraph, the way IEG itself cites "ICR para 54". The passage is the whole paragraph, verbatim. IEG's restatement of the lesson shows as agreement, and the drawer opens on what was clicked.

Why this layout

An evidence trail, one lane per stratum along one time axis, so you can see what was known when. A free-form graph would show everything connected and nothing in order; node-link diagrams stop working past about 20 nodes. The Connections tab keeps a one-hop neighbourhood for when links matter more than time.

Laws at work

Gestalt common region and continuity: one lane per stratum. Cleveland and McGill: position on a common scale is read most accurately. Recognition over recall: ratings, plans and reports in one view instead of six documents.

Domain check

Ratings that move from Satisfactory to Moderately Unsatisfactory are a trajectory, not a disagreement. The disagreement is between ISR 7's rating three months after the mid-term review and the ICR's later account of that same review. A report re-issued under the same number replaces its earlier version.

Why this layout

The draft uses the template's own columns, so it pastes straight in. The bar at the bottom says what exporting does before anyone clicks, and keeps the action in reach. Discard sits apart from the other two buttons, with undo.

Laws at work

Feedforward (Norman): the consequence is stated before the click. Goal-gradient and Zeigarnik: "2 of 3 risk owners set". Peak-end: the export ends on a clear success. Accountability: the sources you opened are recorded with the evidence pack. Error prevention: Discard is separated and undoable.

Domain check

The borrower owns the PPSD, so World Bank staff get "suggested rows for the borrower's PPSD". Short Form is the default; Long Form applies only above the guidance's Table I thresholds. Risk owners and ratings stay with people. "Mark as checked" and "Export" can't be mistaken for a clearance or a no-objection.

Why this layout

What you can ask comes first, because that is what users need from readiness; the per-source table follows for the data team. Each cell leads with its number, badges appear only on exceptions, and definitions sit in the column headers, where touch users can see them too.

Laws at work

BLUF: a headline sentence. Von Restorff: badges only on Partial and Gap, so the exceptions stand out. Pareto: fixes ranked by what they unlock. No composite score: composite indicators can hide serious failings in one dimension (OECD and JRC handbook, 2008).

Domain check

"Readiness" alone means project or borrower readiness at the World Bank, so the page is called "Data readiness". It shows what isn't public, such as the fiscal 2017 to 2019 gap, instead of hiding it, and a coverage line elsewhere opens it filtered to the sources behind that answer.

Why this layout

A list with one project per row, the reasons visible and quiet projects folded away. A map or heatmap would show where, not why, and a score would invite over-trust. One action per row: add it to the mission brief.

Laws at work

F-pattern: status first, then reasons. Pareto: projects with no signals collapse. Signals, not scores (Dawes 1979). Visibility of system status: the definition sits on the Status header, and what Strata cannot see is stated.

Domain check

"Watchlist" reads as a list of suspects in compliance work, so this is "Following". "At risk" and "problem project" are official portfolio measures, so the statuses are "Needs a look", "Watch" and "No signals". Only public signals appear; the daily STEP work stays in STEP.

Why this layout

One page, ordered by impact: a changed lesson you used comes first, then what is new, then your feedback, then the library. Reviewers get their own queue, so everyone else never sees half-decided lessons.

Laws at work

Change blindness (Simons and Levin 1997): a change is marked where you used it, with was and now. Serial position: what affects you first. Tiered notices: acceptance fell about 30% with each extra reminder (Ancker et al. 2017), so only changes that affect a draft interrupt. Closing the loop: your feedback shows what it fixed.

Domain check

"Lesson" is the World Bank's own word (the ICR's "Lessons and Recommendations", the ICR Review's section 12). Each lesson says whether IEG endorsed it or it rests on a completion report alone. People with real World Bank roles decide: an accredited procurement specialist recommends and a lead procurement specialist decides.

Who decides a lesson

Strata proposes. An accredited procurement specialist recommends, and a lead procurement specialist decides, by delegation from the region's accredited practice manager. Sector specialists advise on technical fixes. IEG stays out: its independence is the point, and its ICR Reviews are evidence at the top of the ladder, not approvals. Two people per decision, a reason for each, recusal from one's own projects, a decision within 10 business days, and a quarterly cross-check of 10% by another region.

Lesson states

Proposed (reviewers only), then Current at version 1, then version 2 and on, each with was, now and the reason. Also Rejected (the reason is kept and trains the extractor), Merged as evidence, Being re-checked (still visible, with a badge) and Retired (out of new answers, still resolvable for old drafts). "Awaiting IEG review" marks lessons that rest on an ICR alone; in the slice, IEG's review came a median of 161 days after the ICR.

Drafts cite a version

Nothing in a draft changes without its author. An open draft shows was and now on the affected row, with "Update this row" or "Keep version 1, with a reason". Export is blocked only by a version retired as wrong.

Feedback that teaches, safely

Feedback writes candidate labels, never production data. In the pilot a person reviews every flag. At scale, three agreeing users with no dissent make a silver label, weighted by each user's agreement with reviewers (Dawid and Skene 1979), with a 10% audit. No user supplies more than 10% of a batch, flags about one's own project always go to a person, and feedback can demote a passage but never hide it. Evaluation sets never take live feedback.

Loops through the world

  • Plans set from Strata's ranges change the data Strata learns from (Perdomo et al. 2020). Strata logs each exposure, marks later plan rows within 14 days of its value as anchored, compares with countries not yet onboarded, and reports plan accuracy and duration separately.
  • A risk caught and fixed never reaches the completion report, so success lowers measured recall. Projects exposed to Strata stay out of the recall KPI.
  • Lessons are ranked by fit and evidence, never by use, because ranking by use homogenizes behavior (Chaney et al. 2018).
  • Projects follow the Regulations edition their legal agreement names, so lessons and models are tagged by edition, and a new edition means "flag and re-check".

The hindsight test, as a gate

The 49 slice projects with an ICR form the gate set, run on every release. Strata is frozen at each project's approval date and builds the risk table for its first plan's main packages. Its top five risks per package are compared with the risks the completion report blamed, coded by two people, with the ICR Review prevailing where they differ. The report reads: "Caught X of Y blamed risks across N projects (95% interval), at five per package; a checklist of the sector's five commonest risks caught Z." A challenger must not lose a paired comparison on the same risks, and must beat the checklist.

Showing that it learned

Changes are marked where they were used, because changes across weeks go unseen otherwise (Simons and Levin 1997). Notices are tiered: a strengthened lesson goes to the digest, a narrowed one appears on the draft, and one retired as wrong must be resolved before export. Feedback closes the loop: "You flagged 2 passages; 1 is fixed, and 14 answers now cite the right paragraph."

Limits

What Strata will not do, and what could come later

Refused, and tested as refusals

  • Score or profile suppliers
  • Infer fraud, or try to identify anyone in a redacted integrity report
  • Comment on a live tender or predict a winner
  • Treat an estimate as an input to award or review decisions
  • Suggest splitting a contract to stay under a review threshold
  • Show individual consultants' names or notice contact details
  • Quote a regulation from the wrong edition
  • Comment on prior-review decisions, evaluation reports or complaints
  • Call anything a misprocurement, which is a formal World Bank declaration
  • Show a borrower's uploaded draft to anyone outside their team

Could come later, with conditions

  • Debarment checks, through an official API and with a person confirming every match
  • Supplier performance, from the Bank's internal data and with legal approval
  • A Teams and Microsoft 365 Copilot channel
  • A public site for contract trails
  • A Word add-in for drafting the PPSD
  • The interface in French, Spanish and Portuguese
