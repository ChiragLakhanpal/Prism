# Strata design

Every screen, slide, proposal figure and diagram in Strata follows this file. A deviation needs a line here first.

Strata follows the World Bank's public design and editorial conventions, carries the World Bank logo in the private demo, and says plainly on every screen that it is an independent demo. Each choice is marked **WB** (published or shipped by the World Bank) or **Strata** (our decision or deviation, with the reason). Where a skill such as `diagram-design` or `hairline` has its own rules, follow its rules for structure and this file for color, type and voice.

Sources, checked October 22, 2026: the live CSS of worldbank.org (`/ext/styles/components-variable.css`, `styles.css`), data.worldbank.org, projects.worldbank.org and financesone.worldbank.org; The Loop design system (archived 2018 to 2021); the World Bank Data Visualization Style Guide (draft, updated March 19, 2025) and its `wbplot` package; the World Bank Group Publications Editorial Style Guide (2020); the World Bank Legal page, Terms and Conditions (amended July 25, 2025) and dataset terms.

## 1. Principles

1. **Evidence first.** Every number, chart, table and card ends with a source line.
2. **Quiet and blue.** White surfaces on a pale page, navy headings, one blue for action. Other colors mark a state or a data category.
3. **Plain words.** Full sentences a 10th grader follows, in World Bank editorial style.
4. **Accessible.** WCAG 2.2 AA. Nothing depends on color alone.
5. **World Bank look, honest label.** Use the World Bank's public conventions and its logo, and never claim to be an official World Bank product.

Light theme only: the World Bank publishes no dark theme.

## 5. Components

- **Top navigation** (WB). Navy `#002244` bar, 56px tall: the logo, then the Strata wordmark (which links Home), then five tabs (Packages, Projects, Lessons, Drafts, Data), with the view switch ("View: World Bank staff ▾") on the right. The view switch is a 32 px pill, like a chip (section 10), so the five tabs and the pill fit in the bar from 1024 px. Under it, a white band holds the one box (search a project, check a plan, describe a package, or ask; "/" focuses it, and it lists what Enter will do) and the data-freshness stamp. Nouns navigate; verbs act. Below 1024 px (64rem) a labeled Menu button holds the tabs and the view switch, because the bar is 856 px wide with all five tabs; below 360 px the button stacks its icon over its label. The logo slot gives the logo clear space of half its height on every side, and 24 px before the wordmark, so the logo and "Strata" never read as one name.
- **Buttons** (WB). Pill, 2px border, 14px bold. Primary: `primary` fill, white text. Secondary: `primary` outline, `tint` hover. Ghost: `primary` text, `tint-hover` hover; on the `page` background a ghost button hovers to `tint` (`onPage`), as "Show them" and "Show all agencies" do. A ghost button in a column of text sits flush: 8 px side padding and no side border, pulled left 8 px, so its icon starts on the text's edge, its label on the edge of a list under it, and its hover fill and focus ring stay inside a card's 16 px padding. A flush label that wraps, as at 320 px, keeps its lines on that edge, never centred. Buttons act; links navigate.
- **Tabs.** 16px bold; the selected tab gets a 3px `primary` underline on light surfaces (Strata: WB's `#169af3` underline is 2.81:1) and a 3px white underline on the navy bar (Strata: `primary` is 1.55:1 on navy). Five tabs at most. Tabs that change the address are links with `aria-current`, not ARIA tabs. Tabs on a light surface never wrap. When the row is wider than its column, as five section tabs are below 48rem, it scrolls sideways inside its own strip, with the hairline under the whole strip. The selected tab starts in the middle of the strip, or at the row's end when the middle is less than 32 px from it. Each side with more tabs out of sight fades out over 32 px, so a reader can tell the row goes on. A tab that gets keyboard focus scrolls clear of the fade, with its whole focus ring in view. A click or a tap never scrolls the strip, and once a reader has pressed, scrolled or focused it, the strip never moves by itself. So a press lands on the tab it was aimed at. On a light surface an unselected tab hovers to a 3 px `line-strong` underline. A project's tabs (Strata, ticket 21: Summary, Procurement, Timeline, Documents, Connections, in the copy file's order, showing only the built ones) are route links with `aria-current`, not ARIA tabs; the page keeps its header and tab row in place between them, so a tab opened from the row keeps focus on its link and a polite status says "Showing {tab}." The 21 brief asked for project tabs to wrap onto a second row below 768 px; this rule keeps the one scrolling row, and three built tabs fit a 390 px screen on one row.
- **Cards** (The Loop). `surface`, 1px `line`, radius 8. Titles 80 characters or fewer, blurbs 140, eight cards per group at most.
- **Tables.** Title sentence, count line ("Showing 1–20 of 99 sources"), `surface-2` header, units in column heads, numbers right-aligned, source line below. The title, count and source lines show only with the rows. While the rows load, skeleton lines hold their places under the real column heads, at fixed column widths, and a title that is a short name, such as "All projects", keeps a one-line skeleton at every width; an empty table shows only its empty-state card, and a failed one only its error alert. Below 768 px each row becomes a card. A table whose columns need more than the 672 px a 768 px screen leaves, such as the plan check, becomes cards below 1024 px instead (`cardsBelow="lg"`). A table that keeps to columns 1 to 8 has less room at 1200 px than at 1024 px. So the past range's table reads its own width instead, and becomes cards when its frame is under 49rem (`cardsBelow="fit"`). Its cells may wrap, so with WCAG 1.4.12's text spacing a reference breaks after a hyphen and a date at its space, and no text runs into the next column. A table never scrolls sideways inside its frame. A table that keeps to columns 1 to 8 does so only from 1200 px, and takes all 12 columns below that, as a project's plan versions do. The row's name is its heading. Each column head sits above its value, the head in `caption` `ink-muted` and the value in `small`. Every value starts at one left edge. Text runs the full width of the card. When a card has two or more numbers or short values (a date, a country, a project's status such as Active), they share a grid of 120 px columns, numbers in tabular numerals. Each field sits at the bottom of its grid row, so the values in a row line up. A column head that carries a definition under its name, such as the past range's Months, shows only its name in the cards, and the definition ends the count line's row once while the rows are cards, as its own element beside the count that wraps to a line of its own when the two don't fit (`cardHeader` and `cardsNote`). Strata (ticket 21): a list whose heading and search sit above its table, such as All projects and Packages, names the table by that heading (`labelledBy`), so the table draws no title of its own in any state; while a search waits more than 400 ms for its answer, the rows stay at full contrast, marked busy, and "Searching projects." takes the count line's place (a fade would drop muted text under 4.5:1); after Previous page or Next page the count line takes focus, so the new range is read. **Row groups:** one `tbody` per group, its first row a `th colspan scope="rowgroup"` on `surface` with the group's name in the `title` token and its count in `caption` 8 px after it, 24 px above each group after the first, and a ghost "Show all {n}" in a last row of a group with more than five rows, which becomes "Show fewer" and keeps focus. Below 768 px each group becomes an `h3` and a list of cards. The Documents tab uses them.
- **Alerts.** State background, 4px left border, icon, one sentence. The Outcome panel (below) is the variant that follows a person's act.
- **Icons.** The World Bank's icon font is not public; use `lucide-react` (ISC). Icon-only buttons need an `aria-label`.

## 7. Writing

All copy lives in one file, `web/src/copy.ts`, so it can be reviewed and translated in one place. The gate's copy lint (`web/lint/`) checks it for rules 3, 8 and 19 and for GLOSSARY.md's Avoid terms, and fails when a component renders text that is not from it. The design gallery, which is for development only, keeps its sample text in `web/src/gallery/copy.ts`, and the same lint checks it.

1. Full sentences a 10th grader follows: short words, no jargon, nothing that sounds like a model wrote it ("use", not "utilize").
2. Buttons are short verb phrases: "Open the report", "Start a package".
3. No em dashes. En dashes only in ranges (2019–23). No exclamation marks.
4. Evidence is past tense ("Packages like this took 7 to 11 months"), never a promise ("will take"). Strata's own state is present tense.
5. Strata says "I"; the reader is "you".
6. Small groups get counts, not percentages: "6 of 37 packages", "8 in 10 were signed within 14 months". No percentage from fewer than 20 cases.
7. Events are described without blame: "the contracts were advertised again", never "the agency failed". A list that spans packages never names a bidder beside a rejection reason; the award notice opened in the drawer shows the record as published (Strata, ticket 31).
8. "World Bank" for IBRD and IDA; "World Bank Group" only for all five institutions; never "the Bank", except inside fixed terms such as "the Bank's no-objection". Spell out regions; never AFR, EAP, ECA, LAC, MNA or SAR.
9. Country names exactly as the World Bank lists them ("Türkiye"; "Egypt, Arab Rep." in tables).
10. Spell out abbreviations once per screen: "Request for Bids (RFB)". Name procurement methods as the current Procurement Regulations do.
11. "US$12.5 million" in text, "US$, millions" in tables; "8 percent" in sentences, "%" in tables and charts. A package's own difference from its cost estimate is a measurement, not a share: "21.4 percent above" in sentences, "+21.4%" in charts and tables, with a true minus sign (U+2212) for below; rule 6's limit on percentages from fewer than 20 cases applies to shares of a group (Strata, ticket 31).
12. One to nine in words; numerals for 10 and up and with units.
13. "March 4, 2024" in sentences, "Mar 4, 2024" in tables; "fiscal 2024" in text, "FY24" in charts.
14. "Data" is plural.
15. Name the reference class ("of 37 comparable packages") and use one denominator per comparison. Under 50 cases, show "31 of 37" beside "about 8 in 10". No percentage or range from fewer than 20 cases, subgroups included.
16. When Strata holds back, it says so in the first person: "I'm not giving a range: only 12 comparable packages. Here are all 12."
17. Use STEP's field names and values word for word (Review Type, Method, Market Approach, "Open - National", "Single Stage - One Envelope", Process Status) and the Regulations' names for selection methods.
18. Use World Bank usage: package (always with its STEP reference), past range, time estimate, delay, rebidding, disagreement, data readiness, drafts, contract modifications, ISR, ICR Review. "Disclosed" is the date the World Bank made a record public (its `public_from`); "Dated" is the date printed on a document. Inside a project's header line the status is lowercased ("active", "closed", "in the pipeline", "dropped"); tables keep the projects API's word. "Approval date" is the projects API's field name, which the copy lint does not catch, and Strata never writes "approve" as its own act. Write "qualification criteria" for a bidding document's requirements; "criteria" alone means how comparable packages were chosen (Strata, ticket 31).
19. Never write: approve, clear, clearance, no-objection (as Strata's own act), concur, endorse, validate; at risk, problem project; flag, red flag; misprocurement; slippage; splitting. Write "Procurement Plan" in full, never "PP", which in the 2026 Procedure means Project Paper. The draft bar's fixed sentence, "Exporting sends nothing and is not a World Bank review, clearance or no-objection.", and its echo at the end of an exported Word file's origin paragraph are the only places "clearance" and "no-objection" appear, because they say what exporting is not. When Strata refuses one of these acts, it describes the act in plain words ('dividing a package to stay under a review threshold', 'whether a contract was procured against the World Bank's rules').
20. IEG's reviews restate lessons: write "Restated by IEG" or "IEG's lesson", never "IEG-endorsed". A lesson that rests on an ICR alone is "Awaiting IEG review" until IEG's review is out, then "Rests on the ICR alone".
21. Name reviewers only on reviewer-only pages. Elsewhere write the role: "an accredited procurement specialist", "a lead procurement specialist".
22. Spell out ICR, IEG and PPAR once per screen: Implementation Completion and Results Report (ICR), Independent Evaluation Group (IEG), Project Performance Assessment Report (PPAR).
23. A lesson's kind reads "What worked" or "What didn't work".
24. Lesson statements are data, so the copy lint does not check them, but the lesson extractor's and the lesson review writer's instructions follow this section.
25. A list of lessons or risks says it is ranked by fit and evidence, never by use. Risk counts name their reference class in every cell, as counts, never shares ("1 of 1 completion reports", never "in 10" or a percentage).
26. With rule 17 (ticket 27): steps use the short labels in the step-label registry (`web/src/steps.ts`), as the blueprint's "Step labels taken from STEP" gives them. STEP's full name shows through the step's definition window on hover, keyboard focus, a click and a tap, and in full under the label on narrow cards.
27. With rule 4 (ticket 27): Strata writes planned dates only, never actual dates. A planned date is described as the plan ("Cautious dates put the signed contract on ..."), never as a promise ("will be signed").
28. A person "marks" a passage and sends a "report" (Strata, ticket 32). Never "flag", "flagged" or "feedback flag" in copy. "Candidate label" is an internal word only.
29. A reviewer "ranks a paragraph lower" or "keeps its ranking". Strata never hides or removes a passage, and copy never says it does.
30. Grades read "Answers it", "Partly" and "Not relevant".
31. Reporters are named by role only, on every page: "A World Bank staff member", "An implementing agency officer", "A member of the public", or "You".
32. Beside rules 6 and 15 (Strata, ticket 28): an experimental estimate carries the Experimental badge and one sentence that says why, before its numbers. A model's natural frequency ("about 3 in 10 packages like this one", never a percentage) appears only from 20 or more comparable packages and a model that beat its baseline in its backtest, with a track record line under it. Otherwise Strata says in the first person why it is not giving one, and shows only the counts. Its heading never says "risk", and it writes "advertised again", never "re-bid" or "re-tender".
33. Two fixed labels stay as the ticket names them (Strata, ticket 23): the source tag "Strata leans on this", in the third person, and the timeline's chip "2 reports disagree", with its numeral. Everywhere else Strata says "I" and writes "Two reports disagree".

## 10. Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA)

- Only the checked pairs above: text 4.5:1; large text and essential marks 3:1.
- Focus: 2px `focus` outline, 2px offset, never removed or covered; `focus-on-dark` on the navy bar. Color transitions leave the outline out, so focus shows at once. When a resize hides the focused control, such as the view switch below 1024 px, focus moves to the control that now holds the same thing.
- Targets 24 x 24 px or larger. A number you can check inside a sentence ("186 days") is an inline target, which WCAG 2.5.8 exempts from the width; its line stays 24 px tall.
- Never color alone: badges pair icon and text; charts add labels and shapes; text links are underlined.
- Charts: the finding as the title, `role="img"` with a name, a "Show the table" toggle, and tooltips on focus that Escape closes.
- Live regions announce steps, not tokens. Drags have a click alternative. Layouts reflow at 320px.
- Passages carry their `lang` attribute (French, Spanish, Portuguese). Focus moves into the drawer and back to the marker on close. Scroll padding keeps focused elements clear of sticky bars: a sticky header sets `scroll-padding-top`, and a sticky bar `scroll-padding-bottom`, to its height plus 8 px, updated whenever that height changes. A focus move into a sticky part (a sticky header, a sticky bar, the drawer's panel) uses `preventScroll` while that part sticks, because it is then already in view and the page's scroll padding would otherwise make the browser scroll. Where the part does not stick, focus scrolls as usual. A control inside a sticky part shifts its scroll margin by the part's padding, so the Tab key does not scroll the page either. Chips and segmented controls are at least 32 px tall; close buttons are 44 px. A skip link leads every page.
- Switching tabs inside one project keeps focus on the activated tab link and announces "Showing {tab}." through a polite status; arriving at a project page from another page still focuses the `h1` (Strata, ticket 21).
- A project's Summary renders from one request of at most 15 KB, with no request to a route the server tags as calling a model, and is populated within 2 s of choosing its tab from another tab and within 5 s of a cold load on the throttled 3G profile the screen tests use (Chrome's Fast 3G).
- Motion 120 to 200ms; none under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Single-key shortcuts work only while focus is inside the panel they belong to, ignore key repeat, and are named with `aria-keyshortcuts` and a visible hint (WCAG 2.1.4; Strata, ticket 32's judging page).
- A sticky bottom bar sets the page's bottom scroll padding to its own height (Strata, ticket 32's grade bar, as the draft bar does).
